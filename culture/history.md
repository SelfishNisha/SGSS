# Screen sharing culture

## History

Screensharing began in 2014. First, developers created "hacks" that weren't so blatant (they were somewhat blatant).

The cheating community created modules called triggerbot and clickaimbot, which were well disguised for catching players in games and getting banned.

In the past, people were banned more often in games than for screensharing due to several factors. Most didn't know how to configure or haven't had hacks that featured "Legit hacking" (also known as closet hacking).

In 2014, many people used Resilience, Huzuni (those ahead of their time knew how to configure it a bit better), and others used Synapse Autoclicker.

Before, most people using a hacked client couldn't type .help to appear in the chat. Then, hacked clients started to evolve, and they could type it correctly (this was a test without a screenshare to see if they were using a certain hacked client in 2014).

At that time, most people used Huzuni (1.7.10), Resilience, and the "ghost client" was starting to appear. In this case, it was called "Cringed" here it was a mod that came with Triggerbot, Clickaimbot, and Killaura.
Screen sharing began with this mod.

Triggerbot was blatant, but it wasn't very clear if people were using it for the hits. The old triggerbot worked without "reach," which was 2.9 by default on the mine that hit. It was the same effect if people used autoclicker at 20 cps at that time.

The only fact to know was that there were some bad triggerbots at that time (like Resilence) that didn't have a "click" option to work, so just passing something in their crosshair would cause it to hit and sell the hacker's soul.

Tools like Process Hacker didn't exist back then. What was used to see if someone was using a cheat (it was very bad) was emeditor. It was the most advanced thing in 2015/2016. The verification process was already in place, and even with javaw memory, ours was a pain. You looked for generic things like "triggerbot," "click-aimbot," etc. 

One of the best bypasses back then was hiding the .jar mods.
emeditor was good for mods, it already hacked the client (version). You just had to be careful to check the .jar versions.

and see the file size. Autoclickers were already a headache, they were all installers that could be obtained from sources like SourceForge, and the others were mouse programs like Synapse. They knew where to change the macro file, how to remove traces of macros, etc.

Selfdestruct appeared at the end of 2015
One example was Spooky or even a cracked Vape 2.6. There was a famous SS tool called "cheatsmasher" to detect these cheats, but it wasn't compatible with all PCs.

Examples of cheats in 2016 were Spooky and Spookster (which was a LiteLoader mod) and Serenity B27. This client was very good because the cheat wasn't in the OptiFine version or libraries, but in another library directory. Also around that time there was Incognito, that very old cheat that worked with the Minecraft JVM launcher. Most screenshots were bypasses of this client because they didn't check the JVM being used, but you could still detect those clients with Emeditor.

Then in 2017, ProcessHacker arrived. In 2016, it was enough to know a few things that a cheat generated from files like jnativehook. They were simple things, and they used ExploreTool (it's garbage, and ProcessHacker is an alternative, but you can't scan the string properly).

Someone made a video using ProcessHacker in early 2017, and it was cool. First, they used it to see if there was a clicker running in the background because at that time, people were already using hide-app programs.

In 2016, there was a video from a German guy showing how to cheat using the LabyMod cheat. But in 2018, LabyMod's private clients were really top-notch because they didn't create any .class files and instead coded "legal" .class files. At most, they increased the size of the .class file, and so on.

Then the ScreenShare Community was racking their brains trying to figure out which key to press to open the cheat HUD. It was Shift + R + N + H together (holy fuck).

If you pressed the R key once more, you set the reach to 3.6, and if you pressed it again, 3.9.

They did it that way so as not to be obvious when hiding from jd-gui's search for something like "reach" until there was a way they did it that way, but in processhacker it appeared as "reach". Then the Screenshare community started using iDetect because iDetect and processhacker were the only tools that scanned javaw strings. At that time, the only program that could go undetected by jd-gui were the private labymod clients.
